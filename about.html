I am a mathematician and machine-learning engineer who gradually moved from studying models to studying the systems around them.

Today, I lead the AI Acceleration Team at DxO Labs. We are a small team exploring AI across products, engineering, support, and the way work is organised. We help other teams identify where AI can genuinely extend their work, then build the tools, interfaces, evaluation, and operating loops needed to make that change dependable.

I do not think the interesting question is whether AI replaces a person. The more practical question is how people redesign a system when AI can participate in it—as executor, adviser, and sometimes observer—while humans remain its architects and accountable decision-makers.

How I try to work

Current principles, not finished answers
01

Observe before automating

Start from the real process, its exceptions, and the evidence people already use to make decisions.

02

Build one complete loop

A narrow path from intent to action to feedback teaches more than a broad collection of disconnected demos.

03

Make quality executable

Turn repeated expectations into checks, then keep human attention for ambiguity, architecture, and risk.

04

Share what we learn

Write down the system, the decision, and the result so another team can act without starting from zero.
